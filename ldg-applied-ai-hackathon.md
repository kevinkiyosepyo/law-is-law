## Personal-injury law in 90 seconds

### 1. Someone gets hurt

A car crash is the classic case. They hire an attorney. The attorney only gets paid if the client wins.

### 2. Doctors treat on a promise

Medical providers treat the client now and get paid from the settlement later. It's called a lien. They are betting on the case too.

### 3. The case takes years

Records pile up, insurers negotiate, courts move slowly. The file grows: notes, emails, tasks, documents. Thousands of pages.

### 4. Two sides, one case

- The firm
- The medical providers

Both need to know where the case stands. Right now, neither can see it.

> Small tip: bills (liens) are usually negotiated down at the end, so the client takes home more money.

## The problem

### The system has all the information. It's just not easy to digest.

Every note, email, task and document is captured. Case management systems even give you a dashboard per case. And still, "what's been happening on this case?" means reading everything, or asking a colleague who was there.

**Solved:** Capturing the case. The notes, emails, dates, contacts and documents are already in the system.

**Today's problem:** Digesting it. Turning a live case file into something a human absorbs in ninety seconds. Nobody has solved this well.

### Two sides. Zero visibility.

The firm reconstructs every case by hand. The medical providers treating the client on lien can't see where the case is, or whether there's coverage behind it.

So both fall back to the same tool: email, with no intelligence in it.

## The challenge

Build a solution that acts as a dashboard in order to:

1. Get the internal team members of a firm up to speed on a case.
2. Improve communication and visibility on the case for the medical providers involved in the client's treatment.

Both halves. How you get there is yours.

This goes beyond an AI chat you ask questions of: the point is a visual digestion of everything already in the case, so the attorney and the providers get up to speed without having to know what to ask.

> This is a menu, not a spec. Build the ones you believe in, and build them well.

## Sharing with medical providers

A provider isn't part of the firm, so a lawyer wouldn't share the whole case.

### Share

- Status changes
- Bills and records

### Don't share

- Case strategy
- Anything confidential that isn't relevant to the provider

Not every attorney will choose to share the same information. Not sure where the line is? Ask the attorneys in the room.
