# Kevin Pyo - Car Crash

A fictional personal-injury matter for demonstrating the dashboard. Kevin is a demo character; the incident, people, organizations, records, and amounts are invented. The case is frozen at **October 2, 2026**, so an upcoming event is a scheduled event, not a completed result.

The full case file is [kevin-pyo-car-crash.txt](kevin-pyo-car-crash.txt). It uses the same source format as the other sample cases. The dashboard derives its findings from those records, and each evidence link points back to the original text and line numbers.

## The story in ninety seconds

In the app, open **Evidence** and search **Ninety-second** to find the same short narrative as a dated case note.

On February 11, 2026, Kevin drives through a New Rochelle intersection. He says his light is green. A Parcel Finch delivery van turns left across his path and strikes his car; a following hatchback then hits the rear. The van driver disputes Kevin's account. There is footage, but eleven seconds are missing, the clocks may not agree, and the witnesses did not all have the same view. The file has several accounts of the collision without a settled answer about responsibility.

Kevin fractures both bones in his dominant right forearm and undergoes surgery the next day. Months of treatment follow. The arm improves, but limited rotation and grip still affect his work. A possible further procedure remains a question for his treating clinician. Meanwhile, the record contains a substantial work-loss claim, insurance payments, and medical bills that must be reconciled without counting the same loss twice.

By October 2, counsel is preparing for October 22 mediation. The case has $83,460 in recorded medical charges, but that is not the amount Kevin still owes. The $49,000 claimed work loss is a separate figure with its own supporting-record gaps and a recorded wage-benefit payment to reconcile. A possible excess insurance policy and the final reimbursement amount remain unresolved. The demo's central question is practical: **which missing records and decisions must be resolved to explain this case clearly at mediation?**

## The three threads to follow

| Thread | Why it is complicated | What to open in the demo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collision | Three vehicles, conflicting signal accounts, missing footage, uncertain clock alignment | Witness, dashcam, and vehicle-data records in Evidence |
| Recovery | Surgery is documented, but current restrictions and any further procedure need clarification | Treatment facts and the treating providers' request histories |
| Financial picture | Charges, payments, adjustments, work-loss components, and reimbursement claims describe different things | Recorded financial fields and the reconciliation note |

## A three-minute walkthrough

1. **Select “Kevin Pyo - Car Crash” on the overview.** Read “Case at a glance: Short version” first. Explain what happened and which injuries Kevin reports before discussing any figures. The short version is the entry point; the full history is available in Evidence.
2. **Show what is holding the case up.** Open one of the high-priority items and its source links. Explain the missing item, who has it, and why it matters before mediation. The displayed priority comes from the recorded task due date at the snapshot date.
3. **Open Waiting Room.** Compare an unanswered request with an incomplete response and a received item that needs review. A reply does not necessarily finish the task. The provider's response and the original request should make that distinction understandable.
4. **Open Evidence.** Search for the disputed collision sequence, then open the underlying witness or vehicle-data record. Explain what that source establishes and what it leaves unanswered. Repeat with the financial reconciliation note to distinguish billed charges, payments, claimed work loss, and an unresolved reimbursement claim.
5. **Open Provider sharing.** Choose a treating provider, select a relevant treatment fact and that provider's outstanding request, and inspect the approval preview. Source excerpts are optional. Creating a link is a separate action; the walkthrough can stop at the preview.
6. **Close with the next decision.** Explain which missing records would let counsel prepare a more complete mediation presentation. The demo shows organized evidence and follow-up work; an open task or disputed record does not establish fault or a settlement amount.

## How to tell the story clearly

Keep three threads separate while moving through the app: how the collision happened, how Kevin's recovery affects daily life and work, and which evidence is still needed. Link them only where a record supplies the connection. For example, a treatment restriction can help explain lost work, but the amount of that loss still needs financial support.

Use the dated notes for the longer story. The intake records the initial account; later notes and correspondence add details, challenge early assumptions, and document partial progress. The latest case-conference note explains the position at the snapshot date. Completed tasks remain in the source history but do not inflate the open-task count.

When presenting money, name the kind of figure: a medical charge is not necessarily an unpaid balance, a claimed wage loss is not automatically a documented loss, a coverage limit is not an offer, and a reimbursement assertion is not a final payoff. The fixture keeps these distinctions in its financial fields and reconciliation notes.

**Finances walkthrough:** The October 1 statements show $83,460 billed, $49,000 paid by insurance, $3,000 paid by Kevin, and $18,400 in discounts and write-offs. Open Sable Harbor to see Kevin's September 30 payment of $900, the hospital's $1,500 financial assistance within its $6,000 adjustment, and the four monthly $250 payments covering the remaining $1,000 hospital balance. Across all six providers, $4,010 is assigned to Kevin, $7,000 awaits insurance, and $2,050 has been denied and is under appeal. Those three categories make up the $13,060 remaining statement balance. The older September 25 reconciliation predates Kevin's $900 hospital payment; it is retained as history, not added to the current totals.

Document entries are an inventory. Only the text excerpts actually included in the case file can be opened as source evidence; a listed attachment does not mean a PDF or video exists in the workspace.

## Running the case

The case is added automatically when the app starts. Startup only inserts it if its sample ID is absent; restarting preserves saved snapshots and the other matters. To read the whole story outside the app, open the text export above. Importing that text through **Import matter** creates a separately labeled imported copy, so an ordinary demo does not require importing it again.
